research

My research is primarily in econometrics, monetary economics, and international macroeconomics. More recent projects also cover climate econometrics and economic history. The descriptions below distinguish research manuscripts from ongoing projects; project summaries are concise website descriptions rather than verbatim abstracts from unpublished drafts.

Working papers and research manuscripts

Measuring Sanctions and Their Macroeconomic Transmission

An estimated small-open-economy DSGE model of Iran studies how sanctions constrain oil production and exports, access to imported goods and investment, and the use of external resources. Sanctions intensity is represented by a persistent latent state disciplined by a quarterly chronology and auxiliary measures, while the structural model traces the resulting macroeconomic adjustment. Counterfactual exercises distinguish sanctions-related external restrictions from the domestic frictions that shape their transmission.

Research manuscript; current project title.

Estimating the Impact of Monetary Policy Shocks: A Model Averaging Approach

This project studies how to combine alternative external instruments for monetary policy shocks rather than treating a single proxy as unambiguously correct. It develops and assesses model-averaging and instrumental-variable approaches, paying particular attention to differences across proxy families, instrument strength, exclusion restrictions, and inference in proxy-SVAR and local-projection settings. The empirical application studies U.S. monetary policy transmission.

Research manuscript; title used for conference presentations in 2024–25.

Climate Change: Across Time and Frequencies

With Luís Aguiar-Conraria, Luís F. Martins, and Anthoulla Phella.

Using continuous wavelet methods, this paper investigates how the relationship between temperature anomalies and climate forcings varies across time horizons and historical periods. It separates persistent, low-frequency comovement from fluctuations at shorter frequencies, while allowing the importance of anthropogenic and natural forcings to evolve over time.

Preprint (arXiv)

Deep ReQU Mode-Informed Learning for Global Temperature Forecasting

With Tao Wang.

The paper develops a forecasting approach combining mode-informed statistical learning and deep neural-network methods for global temperature prediction. The aim is to improve the modelling of features of the conditional temperature distribution that need not be captured by conventional mean-based forecasting approaches.

Research manuscript.

Down to the Last of the Mohicans: Disease, Trade and Conflict in the Great Lakes

This economic-history project examines how disease, commercial exchange, and conflict interacted in the Great Lakes region. Its aim is to assess the role of economic incentives and changing trade conditions in the dynamics of intergroup competition and the historical transformation of Indigenous societies, while taking institutional and historical context seriously.

Research project; presented at economic-history conferences.

Asymmetric Effects of Uncertainty Shocks: A FAIR Approach

With Hyejin Park.

The project investigates whether macroeconomic responses to uncertainty shocks are asymmetric and how standard linear specifications can conceal that heterogeneity. It uses a flexible empirical approach to examine differences in transmission across states or shock characteristics, with attention to reliable estimation and inference.

Research in development; presented at the Canadian Economics Association.

Work in progress

Tariff Pass-Through, Pricing Currency, and Monetary Policy in a Small Open Economy

An estimated Canada–rest-of-world DSGE framework studies how import tariffs feed through border prices, imported inputs, retail import prices, and consumer inflation. The project compares producer-, local-, and dominant-currency pricing assumptions, allowing monetary policy and expenditure switching to interact with incomplete pass-through. It also studies the effects of the 2018–19 Canada–U.S. tariff measures.

Provisional descriptive title; ongoing research.

Terms of Trade and Macroeconomic Adjustment in International Panels

This project revisits the empirical relationship between the terms of trade and the business cycle using a large cross-country panel. It investigates heterogeneity, dynamic adjustment, and cross-sectional dependence using dynamic common-correlated-effects and related panel estimators, with explicit comparisons against the standard small-open-economy terms-of-trade literature.

Provisional descriptive title; ongoing research.

Common Persistent Cycles and Long-Run Covariability

This methodological project studies multivariate systems with persistent cyclical components, including oscillations at nonzero frequencies and common movements across series. It develops estimation and model-comparison tools for applications to macroeconomic fluctuations and long-horizon paleoclimate data, while benchmarking cyclical specifications against competing trend and autoregressive processes.

Research in progress; presented as “Common Persistent Cycles” in 2024.

Imperfect Exchange Rate Pass-Through: Empirical Evidence and Monetary Policy Implications

This project studies incomplete exchange-rate pass-through and pricing choices in open economies, combining empirical evidence with structural models of monetary transmission. Particular attention is paid to the consequences of producer-currency, local-currency, and dominant-currency pricing for import prices, inflation, and policy trade-offs.

Research in progress; presented at conferences in 2022 and 2024.

A Two-Bloc Portugal–Euro Area DSGE Model with Housing

An estimated structural framework for Portugal and the euro area incorporates housing and financial linkages to study domestic and common macroeconomic disturbances and the transmission of monetary and macroprudential policies. This work is connected to continuing research collaboration with the Bank of Portugal.

Ongoing research; presented at the Bank of Portugal in June 2026.
